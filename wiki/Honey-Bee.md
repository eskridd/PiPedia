# Honey Bee

The **western honey bee** (*Apis mellifera*) is the insect behind most of the food on your table, and the only one most people can name. One hive holds tens of thousands of workers, a few hundred drones, and a single queen, all running on pheromones and something that looks suspiciously like democracy.

## The waggle dance

When a forager finds a good patch of flowers, she returns to the hive and dances in a figure-eight. The angle of the middle run tells the others the direction relative to the sun; the length of the waggle tells them how far to fly; the enthusiasm of the dance tells them how good it is. Karl von Frisch decoded this language and won a Nobel Prize for it in 1973.

## Why hexagons

Honeycomb cells are hexagonal because hexagons tile a plane with the least wax for the most storage. Mathematicians argued about whether bees had truly found the optimal shape until Thomas Hales proved the honeycomb conjecture in 1999: no other arrangement beats it. The bees got there first by several million years.

## By the numbers

| Measurement | Value |
|---|---|
| Workers per hive | 10,000 to 60,000+ |
| Queen's egg laying | up to about 2,000 eggs per day |
| Honey made per bee in its life | about 1/12 of a teaspoon |
| Flowers visited for 1 kg honey | roughly 4 million |

## A short, busy life

A worker lives 5 to 6 weeks in summer, working her way from nurse maid to comb builder to guard to forager as she ages. Winter bees, raised in autumn, live months instead so the colony can cluster and shiver its way through cold snaps, keeping the queen at the center at a cozy 35 °C.
