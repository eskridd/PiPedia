# Ruby-Throated Hummingbird

The **ruby-throated hummingbird** (*Archilochus colubris*) is a bird that weighs about as much as a 2-cent coin, yet every spring it flies nonstop across the Gulf of Mexico, roughly 800 kilometers of open water, to reach North American nesting grounds. The male's throat patch, or gorget, glows ruby in direct sunlight and looks plain dark when it does not, because the color comes from microscopic feather structure rather than pigment.

## A blur with wings

Hummingbirds are the only birds that can fly backwards, and they hover by sweeping their wings in a figure-eight pattern dozens of times per second. Each wingbeat produces lift on both the downstroke and the upstroke, which is something no other bird manages so well. This lifestyle burns energy at an astonishing rate.

## By the numbers

| Measurement | Value |
|---|---|
| Weight | around 3 grams |
| Wingbeats per second | roughly 50 |
| Heart rate in flight | up to about 1,200 beats per minute |
| Gulf crossing | about 800 km without landing |

## Living on the edge

To fuel all this activity, ruby-throats drink nectar from hundreds of flowers a day and snap up tiny insects for protein. At night, or on cold days, they can drop their body temperature and enter **torpor**, a standby state where their heart rate falls to a few dozen beats per minute and they burn a fraction of the energy they otherwise would. Without this trick, many would not survive the night.

They are fearless for their size and will chase jays, squirrels, and each other away from a good feeder, often dive-bombing intruders in looping displays.
