# Tardigrade

**Tardigrades** (phylum Tardigrada), also called water bears or moss piglets, are eight-legged micro-animals about the size of a poppy seed. They live in moss, lichen, leaf litter, and ocean sediment on every continent, including Antarctica, and they are famous for being nearly impossible to kill.

## The tun

When their environment dries out, a tardigrade curls into a barrel shape called a tun, expels almost all its water, and shuts its metabolism down to effectively zero. In this state, called **cryptobiosis**, it can survive:

- temperatures from just above absolute zero to about 150 °C
- the vacuum and radiation of open space (confirmed by experiments on the outside of the ISS)
- pressures six times higher than the deepest ocean trench
- decades without water; one specimen revived after roughly 30 years as a dried museum sample

Add water again, and the tardigrade simply walks away.

## Not immortal, though

Tardigrades are tough, not invincible. They die from ordinary things: being eaten (nematodes and other tardigrades eat them), prolonged heat without drying, and physical crushing. Their superpower is specifically surviving environments that would kill most life instantly, by opting out of those environments entirely.

They were first described in 1773 by Johann August Ephraim Goeze, who called them kleiner Wasserbär, the little water bear. The name stuck.
