# Sea Otter

The **sea otter** (*Enhydra lutris*) spends nearly its whole life in the cold North Pacific, has no blubber to keep warm, and manages anyway with the densest fur of any animal on Earth: up to about 100,000 hairs per square centimeter. That is more hair than many dogs have on their entire body, packed into a patch the size of your thumbnail.

## Tool use with snacks

Sea otters are one of the few animals that use tools for eating. They carry a favorite rock in a loose pocket of skin under each foreleg and use it to crack open sea urchins, clams, and abalone while floating on their backs. Some otters keep the same prized rock for years.

They also wrap themselves in kelp while resting so they do not drift out to sea, and they famously hold hands in groups called rafts to stay together.

## The keystone that holds a forest up

Where sea otters thrive, kelp forests thrive too. Otters eat sea urchins; without otters, urchins mow down entire kelp forests and leave urchin barrens behind. Biologists call the otter a **keystone species**: its presence shapes the whole ecosystem, from fish nurseries to the carbon the kelp pulls out of the water.

## By the numbers

| Measurement | Value |
|---|---|
| Weight | 14 to 45 kg |
| Fur density | up to ~100,000 hairs per cm² |
| Metabolism | 2 to 3 times that of a similar-sized land mammal |
| Daily food intake | 20 to 25% of body weight |

## Warm from within

With no blubber, sea otters stay warm by burning fuel constantly. Their metabolic furnace runs two to three times hotter than expected for their size, which is why an adult eats a quarter of its body weight every day and why orphaned pups need near-constant care.
