# Red Fox

The **red fox** (*Vulpes vulpes*) is the most widespread wild carnivore on Earth, native across the entire Northern Hemisphere and introduced to Australia. It thrives in forests, deserts, tundra, suburbs, and city parks, which says a lot about its defining trait: flexibility.

## The magnetic pounce

A fox mousing in deep snow performs one of nature's strangest hunting rituals. It creeps forward, tilts its head, then leaps high and dives nose-first into the snow. Research suggests the fox is not just listening but also sensing the Earth's magnetic field: successful attacks happen far more often when the fox jumps toward the northeast, as if it were using the field as a rangefinder to judge depth through the snow. Nobody fully understands how yet.

## Life in red

Despite the name, red foxes come in shades from pale yellow to nearly black (the "silver fox"), always with white-tipped tails. They live in family groups where grown daughters often stay to help raise the next litter, while young males leave in autumn to find their own territory.

Their reputation for cunning is well earned. Foxes cache surplus food in scattered hiding places, remember thousands of them, and have been observed pretending to cache food to deceive onlookers.
