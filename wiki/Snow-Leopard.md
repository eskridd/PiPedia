# Snow Leopard

The **snow leopard** (*Panthera uncia*) lives higher than almost any other big cat, among the cliffs and ridgelines of twelve Central Asian countries, from Afghanistan to Nepal and western China. Locals call it the ghost of the mountains, because even researchers who spend years in its habitat may never see one.

## Built for thin air and stone

Everything about the snow leopard is oversized. Its tail can be nearly as long as its body, used as a counterbalance during leaps across rock faces and wrapped around itself like a scarf at -40 °C. Enormous paws work like snowshoes, dense fur grows even on the nose, and pale smoke-grey markings dissolve a cat against granite into nothing.

Unlike lions, tigers, and leopards, snow leopards cannot roar. Their voice box lacks the specialized tissue for it, so they express themselves in chuffs, mews, and a seasonal moaning yowl that echoes across valleys.

## By the numbers

| Measurement | Value |
|---|---|
| Weight | 22 to 55 kg |
| Tail length | 80 to 105 cm |
| Home range | up to about 1,000 km² for males |
| Living altitude | up to about 5,500 m |

## A solitary hunter

Snow leopards hunt blue sheep, ibex, and marmots, stalking downhill from above in bounds of up to 15 meters. They are so elusive that population estimates rely mostly on camera traps, genetic samples from scat, and tracks in snow. The best current guess puts the wild population somewhere between roughly 4,000 and 6,500 adults.
