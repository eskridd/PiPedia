# Emperor Penguin

The **emperor penguin** (*Aptenodytes forsteri*) is the tallest and heaviest of all penguins, and the only one that breeds during the Antarctic winter. It stands about 1.2 meters tall, can weigh over 40 kilograms before breeding season, and spends its entire life on and around sea ice.

## Breeding in the dark

Emperors trek up to 100 kilometers inland to their colonies in March and April, just as the Antarctic sun is leaving. After the female lays a single egg, she hands it to the male and walks back to the open ocean to feed. The male balances the egg on his feet under a fold of skin called a brood pouch and incubates it through months of polar night, storms, and temperatures below -50 °C, eating nothing the entire time.

When chicks hatch, males can produce a curd-like secretion from the throat to feed them until the females return.

## The huddle

To survive winds that can exceed 150 km/h, thousands of emperors pack into rotating huddles. Birds on the cold outside edge slowly shuffle toward the warm center while warmer birds drift outward, so no individual freezes. The whole formation moves like a very slow, very fluffy fluid.

## Divers of the ice

Emperor penguins hunt fish, squid, and krill underwater, and they are extraordinary divers. Satellite-linked recorders have logged dives deeper than 500 meters lasting over 20 minutes, among the deepest recorded for birds. Their bones are solid rather than air-filled, which reduces buoyancy and makes deep diving easier.
