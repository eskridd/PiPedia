# Barn Owl

The **barn owl** (*Tyto alba*) is the ghost you sometimes glimpse over a field at dusk. It has a pale, heart-shaped face, long legs, and golden upper wings, and it hunts small rodents in almost total silence. Unlike most owls it does not hoot; its call is a long, raspy screech that startled farmers once attributed to anything but an owl.

Barn owls live on every continent except Antarctica, which makes them one of the most widely distributed land birds. They nest in tree hollows, caves, church towers, and, as the name suggests, barns.

## Hunting by ear alone

A barn owl's flattened face works like a satellite dish, funneling sound toward asymmetrically placed ears: one sits higher than the other. By comparing the tiny difference in arrival time and volume, the owl can pinpoint a mouse rustling under grass or even snow, then strike without ever seeing it. Experiments have shown captive owls hitting hidden targets in complete darkness using sound alone.

## Silent flight

The leading edge of a barn owl's wing feathers is lined with fine combs and fringes that break up turbulent airflow. The result is flight so quiet that prey often never hears it coming. Researchers studying quiet drones and fans keep returning to these feathers for inspiration.

## Pellets

Like many owls, barn owls swallow prey whole and later cough up a pellet of bones and fur. Scientists dissect these pellets to reconstruct local food webs, which makes the barn owl one of the most useful pest controllers humans never had to hire.
