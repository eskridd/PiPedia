# Peregrine Falcon

The **peregrine falcon** (*Falco peregrinus*) is, by every measurement we have, the fastest animal on the planet. In level flight it cruises along like any respectable bird of prey. When it folds its wings and drops into a hunting dive called a stoop, it has been clocked at more than 380 km/h.

You can find peregrines almost anywhere on Earth. They live on every continent except Antarctica, from Arctic tundra to city centers, where skyscrapers stand in nicely for cliff faces and pigeons replace wild prey.

## The stoop

A peregrine climbs high above its target, tucks its wings back, and falls. Small bony baffles inside the nostrils keep the airway open under crushing pressure, and a third eyelid called a nictitating membrane clears the eyes like windshield wipers. Engineers have studied both features when designing aircraft intakes and cockpit visors.

## Hunting and diet

Peregrines mostly hunt other birds in midair: pigeons, doves, songbirds, occasionally small ducks. The strike itself is usually a single blow with a half-closed foot, either snapping the spine or stunning the prey, which is then caught before it hits the ground.

## By the numbers

| Measurement | Value |
|---|---|
| Recorded dive speed | over 380 km/h |
| Typical level flight | about 100 km/h |
| Eyesight | roughly 8 times sharper than human |
| Wingspan | 80 to 120 cm |

## A comeback story

Widespread use of the pesticide DDT thinned eggshells and crashed peregrine populations across North America and Europe by the 1960s. After DDT was banned and captive-bred birds were released in cities, numbers recovered so well that the species was removed from the US endangered list in 1999. It remains one of conservation's clearest success stories.
