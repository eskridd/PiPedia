# Blue Whale

The **blue whale** (*Balaenoptera musculus*) is the largest animal that has ever lived, as far as we know, larger than any dinosaur yet discovered. Adults reach about 30 meters and can approach 180 tonnes. Its heart alone weighs around 180 kg, its tongue weighs as much as an elephant, and a human toddler could crawl through its largest blood vessels.

## Eating by the tonne

All that bulk is built on some of the ocean's smallest creatures. In summer feeding grounds a blue whale engulfs enormous mouthfuls of krill, then filters them through baleen plates. A good day can mean four tonnes of krill, roughly the calorie equivalent of what it takes to sustain several African elephants, in one animal.

## The loudest voice in the ocean

Blue whales produce low-frequency calls that reach levels near 188 decibels, making them among the loudest animals on Earth. Because low frequencies travel enormous distances underwater, these calls may carry across entire ocean basins, letting whales that never meet still keep track of each other.

## By the numbers

| Measurement | Value |
|---|---|
| Length | up to about 30 m |
| Weight | up to about 180 tonnes |
| Heart | around 180 kg, beating 2 to 8 times per minute on deep dives |
| Calf growth | about 90 kg gained per day while nursing |
| Krill per day in season | up to ~4 tonnes |

## From hunted to hopeful

Commercial whaling reduced the Antarctic blue whale population from over 200,000 to perhaps a few hundred individuals by the 1970s. International protection arrived in 1966, and populations are now slowly climbing, though they remain a small fraction of their original size. Seeing one today is rare enough that researchers celebrate every sighting.
