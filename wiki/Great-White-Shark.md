# Great White Shark

The **great white shark** (*Carcharodon carcharias*) is the ocean's most recognizable predator, and quite possibly its most misunderstood. It grows to 6 meters, hunts seals with explosive bursts from below, and has survived every catastrophe that wiped out the dinosaurs, yet a person is far more likely to be injured by a cow, a ladder, or their own bathtub than by this fish.

## A living fossil in the best sense

Sharks as a lineage predate trees, Saturn's rings being as old as they are, and the existence of North America. Great whites specifically appeared around 11 million years ago, but their ancestors were patrolling the seas over 400 million years ago. Five mass extinctions later, they are still here.

## Built like a sensor array

A great white navigates its world through detectors humans barely have analogies for. Its snout is packed with **ampullae of Lorenzini**, jelly-filled pores that sense the faint electric fields every living muscle produces; a heartbeat can be detected from meters away through darkness. It also feels vibrations through a lateral line along its body and can smell blood at concentrations of one part per million or better.

## Warm-blooded-ish

Unlike most fish, great whites keep major swimming muscles several degrees warmer than the surrounding water using a heat-exchanging network of blood vessels. The warm muscles deliver the short, violent acceleration needed to breach on seals, launching the shark fully out of the water off South Africa's coast.
