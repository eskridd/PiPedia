# Common Octopus

The **common octopus** (*Octopus vulgaris*) is, by most measures of brainpower per body weight, the smartest invertebrate on Earth. It has roughly 500 million neurons, which puts it in the neighborhood of dogs, and about two thirds of them are not in its head at all. They are distributed through its eight arms, which means each arm can taste, touch, and to a surprising extent act on its own.

## A mind with eight hands

Octopus arms solve problems locally: one arm can explore a crevice while the rest of the animal does something else. In laboratories octopuses open screw-top jars, remember which of several containers hold food, use coconut shells as portable shelters, and recognize individual humans, squirting water at the keepers they dislike.

## Master of disguise

An octopus is colorblind, yet it matches the color, texture, and brightness of its surroundings almost instantly. Skin packed with millions of pigment sacs (chromatophores), reflective cells, and muscular bumps lets it impersonate rocks, coral, and even moving plants. Exactly how a colorblind animal achieves color-matching is still an open research question; one hypothesis is that skin cells themselves may detect light.

## By the numbers

| Measurement | Value |
|---|---|

| Escape gap | anything larger than its beak |
| Lifespan | usually 1 to 2 years |

## Short life, big exit

Octopuses are jet-propelled (they siphon water out fast when fleeing), squeeze through impossibly small holes since they have no bones, and live briefly. A common octopus that survives to reproduce is doing well at age two. Aquariums know them as escape artists with strong opinions about lids.
