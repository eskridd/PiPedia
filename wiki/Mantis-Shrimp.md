# Mantis Shrimp

The **mantis shrimp** (order Stomatopoda) is a small, aggressively colorful crustacean that punches with the acceleration of a bullet and sees colors you cannot imagine. There are around 450 species, split into spearers, which stab soft prey with barbed limbs, and smashers, which bludgeon crabs and snails. The smashers are the famous ones.

## The fastest punch in the ocean

A peacock mantis shrimp closes its club-shaped limb so fast that the water around it boils. The strike reaches roughly 23 meters per second, generates forces over 1,000 times its body weight, and leaves behind **cavitation bubbles** that collapse with a flash of light and heat, hitting the prey a second time. Aquarium keepers call large specimens thumb-splitters for practical reasons: they can crack glass.

The secret is a biological spring. The shrimp slowly compresses a saddle-shaped structure in its joint, latches it, then releases everything in under three milliseconds. Engineers study this mechanism for tougher ceramics and new robotics.

## Eyes beyond imagining

Mantis shrimps have the most complex eyes in the animal kingdom, with 12 to 16 photoreceptor types where humans have 3. They see ultraviolet, detect several kinds of polarized light invisible to us, and move their eyes independently like tiny turret scanners. Oddly, they are worse than humans at telling similar colors apart; their brains seem to process color as hard-wired categories rather than fine comparisons.
