# Common Raven

The **common raven** (*Corvus corax*) is a jet-black bird the size of a red-tailed hawk, found across the Northern Hemisphere from deserts to Arctic tundra. It is also one of the smartest animals we know of, and quite possibly the only bird that has ever talked its way out of trouble with a human.

## A mind in feathers

Ravens plan for future events, which was long considered a uniquely human trait. In controlled experiments they select a stone tool now so they can get food later, and refuse an immediate treat when a better one is promised if they can wait. They use gesture-like communication, play in what looks like pure joy (snowboarding down snowy rooftops is documented), and hold grudges against specific humans who have cheated them.

They also mimic speech, wolf howls, car alarms, and dripping taps.

## Life among ravens

Ravens mate for life and defend large territories together. Young non-breeding birds form roaming flocks with their own social order. Pairs build bulky stick nests on cliffs and tall trees and can raise broods there year after year. Their vocabulary of calls includes at least 15 distinct signals researchers have catalogued, each with its own context.

## Culture and myth

Because ravens are black, clever, and comfortable around the dead, nearly every culture gave them a role in its stories: Odin's spies Huginn and Muninn, the trickster-creator of Pacific Northwest nations, the guardians of the Tower of London. The legends exaggerate, but not by much.
