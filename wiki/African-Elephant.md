# African Elephant

The **African elephant** (*Loxodonta africana* and *L. cyclotis*) is the largest land animal alive and one of the most socially complex. A big savanna bull can stand 4 meters at the shoulder and weigh over 6 tonnes, yet herds move with a quietness that still surprises researchers.

## Families run by grandmothers

Herds are matriarchal. Related females and their calves travel together under the leadership of the oldest cow, whose memory of distant waterholes and dangerous places can decide whether the family survives drought. Males leave the herd as teenagers and live mostly alone or in loose bachelor groups.

Elephants mourn their dead, comfort distressed herd members with touch and low rumbles, cooperate on tasks that require two ropes pulled at once, and recognize themselves in mirrors. Few species pass all the tests we throw at animal minds. Elephants pass them routinely.

## Talking below hearing

Much of elephant conversation happens in **infrasound**, rumbles so low in frequency that humans feel them rather than hear them. These calls travel through the air and ground for many kilometers, letting separated families coordinate movements across the savanna at night.

| Measurement | Value |

## Engineering the landscape

Elephants knock down trees, dig waterholes that other animals use, and spread seeds in their dung. Biologists call them ecosystem engineers: where elephants thrive, entire habitats take their shape.
